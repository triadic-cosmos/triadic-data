\chapter{The Mirror Beyond the Laboratory}

Dr. Lanyon had never placed much faith in stories of talking animals, enchanted gardens, or impossible dreams. For that reason he paused in astonishment when a white rabbit carrying a silver pocket watch hurried through the door of his laboratory.

“I shall be far too late,” the creature muttered nervously.

Across the room, Mr. Utterson lowered a stack of papers and stared.

“A rabbit,” he said carefully, “with a watch.”

The animal rushed toward a tall mirror standing against the wall. To the men's amazement, the glass shimmered like disturbed water. Without hesitation, the rabbit leapt through and vanished.

Lanyon stepped forward.

“This is impossible.”

“Then we should investigate it immediately,” replied Utterson.

Together they crossed the threshold.

On the other side stretched a moonlit garden unlike any place they had ever seen. Roses whispered among themselves. Strange birds sang melancholy songs from silver trees. A blue caterpillar sat upon a weathered gravestone and released rings of smoke into the night air.

From somewhere in the mist came a familiar laugh.

The Cheshire Cat slowly appeared, beginning with his grin.

“You're late,” said the cat.

“Late for what?” asked Utterson.

“For discovering that dreams and nightmares share the same address.”

Before either man could reply, footsteps echoed through the darkness.

A lone figure emerged beneath the trees.

At one moment it resembled Dr. Jekyll.

A moment later it bore the features of Hyde.

Then the face changed yet again.

The figure smiled.

“Welcome,” it said softly, “to the place where no mask can remain hidden for long.”

\chapter{Alice and the Second Shadow}

Alice awoke upon a fog-filled street in London.

Tall gas lamps glowed through the mist, casting long shadows over the cobblestones. Nothing looked familiar, yet nothing seemed entirely strange.

A brass sign hung beside a narrow doorway.

Naturally, Alice entered at once.

The laboratory contained shelves crowded with bottles, notebooks, and curious apparatus. On a small table stood a glass vial bearing a handwritten label.

Do not drink.

Alice frowned.

She had always considered such instructions extremely suspicious.

Without further thought she drank the contents.

Immediately the room stretched upward.

The floor drifted away.

The walls seemed miles apart.

Then she noticed something far more alarming.

A second shadow stood beside her.

Alice looked down.

Her own shadow remained attached to her feet.

The newcomer did not.

It moved independently.

“Who are you?” Alice asked.

The shadow tilted its head.

“Perhaps I am everything you try not to be.”

Its voice sounded strangely familiar.

Not unpleasant.

Merely unsettling.

The shadow's smile widened.

For a brief instant it reminded Alice of the Cheshire Cat.

Then the expression changed.

It became colder.

Crueler.

Almost human.

“You may call me Hyde,” it whispered.

Outside, thunder rolled across the rooftops.

Alice suddenly wished she had obeyed the label.

\chapter{The Queen of Mist}

The fog rolled across the park like pale smoke.

Alice wandered through the darkness, following a narrow path beside a silent pond. The moon hung low above the trees, its reflection trembling upon the water.

After several minutes she came upon the Red Queen.

At least, it appeared to be the Red Queen.

Her crown was tarnished.

Her dress was torn.

And her shadow moved independently from her body.

“You seem unwell,” said Alice cautiously.

The Queen sighed.

“Someone has stolen my better qualities.”

“Who?”

The Queen raised a gloved finger and pointed across the pond.

A solitary gentleman stood among the mist.

His tall silhouette never moved.

Only his eyes seemed alive.

“Edward Hyde,” said the Queen.

The very name disturbed the landscape.

Ripples spread across the water.

The wind grew colder.

The trees leaned inward as though listening.

“What does he want?” Alice asked.

“What he has always wanted.”

“And what is that?”

The Queen's expression hardened.

“A world in which nobody remembers who they truly are.”

The fog thickened.

Alice stepped backward.

Somewhere in the distance a cat began to laugh.

The sound echoed across the park.

At the same moment Hyde smiled.

And Wonderland became just a little darker.
